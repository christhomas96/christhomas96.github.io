the hermetic axiom "as above, so below" is usually treated as mystical shorthand — something you put on a poster. but spend enough time in systems thinking and you start to notice it's making a precise structural claim: patterns self-replicate across scales of organisation. the same shapes recur. the micro mirrors the macro.

complexity theory says roughly the same thing. emergence, feedback loops, attractor states — these are formalisms for describing how order arises from noise, how a system develops properties that weren't present in its components. the alchemists said you couldn't understand gold without understanding the sun. the cyberneticists said you couldn't understand a system without understanding its environment. same argument, different costumes.

what i find interesting is that *ritual* — the core technology of magick — is essentially a practice of directing attention deliberately. you're not summoning demons. you're engineering your own pattern of perception. you're deciding, in advance, what to notice and amplify. this is also what good product strategy is. you pick a frame. you commit to it. the frame shapes what data you see, which shapes what you build.

none of this collapses the distinction between empirical science and occult practice. but i think the dismissal is too easy. the question isn't whether the universe responds to ritual — it's whether the practitioner does. and that's not mystical at all. that's just psychology with better aesthetics.
