there's a kind of life that doesn't fit the stories we're given for it.

not the triumph story, where the hard years were secretly preparing you for something. not the tragedy story, where one wrong turn at eighteen cost you the life you were supposed to have. not the redemption story, where grinding hard enough eventually buys you out.

just a life that kept moving forward on whatever road was there when a road was needed. accumulating weight. accumulating skill. accumulating quiet losses that never got a funeral.

the cruelty isn't the hardship. it's that hardship doesn't resolve into meaning on its own.

frost knew this, and everyone misreads him for it. the two roads in the yellow wood were the same road. he says so in the poem — "really about the same," "equally lay." the less-travelled one is invented later, *with a sigh*, when he's old enough to need the story to mean something. meaning isn't found at the fork. it's assigned at the desk, years after, by someone tired enough to need it.

most of us aren't carrying a wrong turn. we're carrying the ordinary friction of being a specific person in a specific place, making decisions with the information available, ending up somewhere we didn't picture at twenty-two. the gap between the imagined life and the lived one is real, and it's heavy, and it doesn't go away by being reframed. but it isn't evidence that you failed. it's evidence that you're alive and paying attention.

the things you carry are real. the things you've built on top of them are also real — and more load-bearing than the scarcity in your head lets you feel. the house is standing. the siding has a crack. those are different facts, and confusing them is how people spend decades bracing for a collapse that isn't coming.

here's the part nobody tells you: the work isn't escaping the cage or finding the keys. it's noticing, on a normal wednesday, that the door has been open for a while, and walking out without needing the moment to mean anything.

that's the whole thing. the rest is just weather.
